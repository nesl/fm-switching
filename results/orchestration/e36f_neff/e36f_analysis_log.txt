5. CIRCULARITY OBJECTION (anticipate and state defense)

  Objection: neff_ranked ranks candidates by exactly the quantity that Part A
  identified as determining capacity (N_eff = min(N_mem, N_accel)). Passing
  S2 is therefore close to true by construction: the policy selects the
  representation that Part A says maximizes sessions supported, and the
  simulation confirms that more sessions admitted ≈ higher both_met.

  Defense:
  (a) The contribution is the selection criterion, not a search procedure.
      neff_ranked is not 'trained' to maximize the outcome metric; it implements
      a closed-form formula derived from the capacity analysis. The formula is
      new and non-obvious: no deployed system scores state-holding choices by
      min(floor(kv_cap/kv_per_session), floor(budget/(maint+serve))).
      Standard footprint criteria (Q/kv_bytes) ignore the maintenance cost term.

  (b) S3 provides independent evidence. footprint_ranked is measurably worse
      in the accel-bound regime across all kv_cap values at ti=5s. This gap
      persists even if neff_ranked is indistinguishable from always_full in
      that regime — it shows the CRITERION matters, not which particular policy
      happened to implement it.

  (c) The distinguishability analysis (item 2 above) honestly states whether
      neff_ranked's outcome differs from every fixed policy. If it does not,
      the policy is reported as a regime-adaptive design rule ('choose full
      below ti~6s at kv=9GiB, window above it'), not as an autonomous policy.
      This is a legitimate and reportable outcome.

  (d) maintenance_aware (the prior policy) passes S3 but fails S2 in 2 cells.
      neff_ranked is tested against the same S2 condition. The comparison
      between the two policies (same causal chain, different scoring formulas)
      is a controlled test of whether incorporating the memory term in the score
      fixes the diagnosed failure mode.
