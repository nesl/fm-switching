BATCHING ANALYSIS — sum200 only (full/win10 use append, not regenerate)
Batching speedup B: effective maint_sum200 = MAINT_SUM200_MS / B
Sweep B in {1, 2, 4, 8, 16}

Context: sum200 regenerates 160-token summaries — embarrassingly batchable.
full/win10 append to KV cache (single forward pass); batching gain is marginal.
This analysis only changes the sum200 accelerator column.

Inversion analysis: at what B does sum200 recover to win10 level at ti=5s?
  B=1  : sum200 N_accel(5s)=  0, win10 N_accel(5s)=6 → sum200 < win10
  B=2  : sum200 N_accel(5s)=  1, win10 N_accel(5s)=6 → sum200 < win10
  B=4  : sum200 N_accel(5s)=  3, win10 N_accel(5s)=6 → sum200 < win10
  B=8  : sum200 N_accel(5s)=  6, win10 N_accel(5s)=6 → sum200 ≥ win10 — PARTIAL INVERSION COLLAPSE
  B=16 : sum200 N_accel(5s)= 12, win10 N_accel(5s)=6 → sum200 ≥ win10 — PARTIAL INVERSION COLLAPSE

Does the FULL inversion collapse at any B? (requires accel order = memory order)
  Memory order: sum200(1053) > win10(23) > full(8)
  Accel order needs: sum200 > win10 > full
  But full always has N_accel = floor(ti/(66+59)ms) — lowest maint, highest N_accel.
  At ti=5s: full N_accel=40, win10 N_accel=6
  Full always beats win10 in accel. The full/win10 inversion never collapses.
  The fundamental inversion (full: worst memory, best accel) persists for any finite B.
  B* = ∞ for full inversion. Partial collapse (sum200 recovers past win10) at B≈8 (ti=5s only).

Does non-monotonicity persist? (win10 winning min(N_mem, N_accel))

Three-way non-monotone (win10 as argmax across full/win10/sum200):
  B=1  : ti=15s: (s200=2, w10=20, full=8)  ti=30s: (s200=5, w10=23, full=8)  ti=60s: (s200=10, w10=23, full=8)
         → win10 still argmax at ti>=15s
  B=2  : ti=15s: (s200=5, w10=20, full=8)  ti=30s: (s200=10, w10=23, full=8)  ti=60s: (s200=20, w10=23, full=8)
         → win10 still argmax at ti>=15s
  B=4  : ti=15s: (s200=10, w10=20, full=8)  ti=30s: (s200=20, w10=23, full=8)  ti=60s: (s200=40, w10=23, full=8)
         → win10 loses argmax at ti=60s (s200=40 > w10=23)
  B=8  : ti=15s: (s200=19, w10=20, full=8)  ti=30s: (s200=39, w10=23, full=8)  ti=60s: (s200=78, w10=23, full=8)

         → win10 loses argmax at ti=15s (s200=37 > w10=20)
  Three-way non-monotone holds for B <= 2.
  Collapses first at B=4, ti=60s (sum200 overtakes win10 in N_eff).

Two-way non-monotone (full vs win10 only — LoCoMo admissible representations):
  full and win10 both use KV-append (single forward pass); batching provides no speedup.
  N_accel(full, ti=5s)=40 always > N_accel(win10_amz, ti=5s)=6 for any B.
  Win10 KV-bound at 23 sessions; full KV-bound at 8 sessions. At ti>=17s: win10 wins.
  Two-way non-monotone (win10 argmax in {full, win10}) holds for ALL finite B.

[NOTE] For LoCoMo at q_slo=0.20: sum200 is inadmissible (Q=0.12 < 0.20).

REFRESH POLICY AXIS — sum200 accelerator demand vs deadline-miss rate

Policies: eager (every turn), periodic-K (K∈{2,5,10}), lazy (on-demand).
  eager/periodic-K: regeneration is PROACTIVE → TTFT = serve_ms = 32ms (no deadline miss).
  lazy: regeneration is ON THE QUERY PATH → TTFT = 5822+32 = 5854ms > 1000ms SLO.

[ASSUMPTION A2a] Stale trigger rate R(K): for LoCoMo (dense-incompressible),
  evidence is approximately uniformly distributed across turns. After K stale
  turns, fraction of queries needing evidence from those turns ≈ K/22 (E33a).
  This is a LOWER BOUND on deadline-miss rate — recency effects may make it higher.

[NOTE] E27 periodic-K measurements are flagged as buggy in E27/E32 open items.
  Those numbers are NOT used here. The trigger rates below derive solely from
  [ASSUMPTION A2a] and are clearly labelled.

  policy          mode         accel_demand/turn   deadline_miss   N_accel(5s)  N_accel(60s)
  eager           proactive                 5822            0.0%             0            10
  periodic-2      proactive                 2911            0.0%             1            20
  periodic-5      proactive                 1164            0.0%             4            50
  periodic-10     proactive                  582            0.0%             8            97
  lazy(K=1)       on-demand                  265            4.5%            16           202
  lazy(K=2)       on-demand                  529            9.1%             8           106
  lazy(K=5)       on-demand                 1323           22.7%             3            44
  lazy(K=10)      on-demand                 2646           45.5%             1            22

Frontier interpretation:
  eager:      zero deadline misses; worst accelerator utilization (5822ms/turn)
  periodic-K: zero deadline misses; K× better accelerator; quality degrades
             when summary is stale (K−1 turns per cycle have outdated context)
  lazy:       deadline misses at rate R; best accelerator use; quality miss = R

  For LoCoMo at q_slo=0.20: sum200 inadmissible regardless (Q=0.12 < 0.20).
  The refresh-policy axis applies to: EgoSchema (admissible), or q_slo=0 cells.
  For EgoSchema (Q(sum200)=0.483 >= 0.20): lazy at K=5 → 22.7% deadline misses.
  Proactive eager dominates lazy on deadline misses; periodic-10 is the
  Pareto-best if quality degradation from K=10 staleness is tolerable.

  Refresh-policy boundary for three-way non-monotone (including sum200):
    periodic-10: sum200 N_accel(5s)=8 vs win10 N_accel(5s)=6 → sum200 beats
    periodic-10: sum200 N_accel(60s)=97 vs win10 N_eff(30s)=23 → sum200 beats
    At periodic-10, sum200 beats win10 at all turn intervals — three-way non-monotone collapses.

  Refresh-policy boundary for two-way non-monotone (full vs win10, LoCoMo):
    full/win10 use KV-append — no per-session regeneration, no batching speedup.
    TTFT = serve_ms (proactive, eager always). No refresh policy axis for these two.
    The two-way non-monotone result is insensitive to refresh policy.

  Summary: the non-monotone result has two scopes:
    (i) TWO-WAY (full vs win10): holds for all B and all refresh policies. LoCoMo headline.
    (ii) THREE-WAY (sum200 included): collapses at B=4 (ti=60s) or periodic-5 refresh (ti=60s).
    The paper claim is stated as the capacity relationship; (i) is the load-bearing version.
