# StudyE9c-challenge-metric: the Nexar challenge's own metric, on our validation clips

E-id: StudyE9c-challenge-metric (`research/EXPERIMENTS.md`, pre-registered 2026-11-12 before any computation). **Offline analysis of committed results. CPU only on the flash host: no GPU, no model server, no inference.** Scores are the continuous log P(Yes) read from StudyD14b's committed 0.25-s trace; no literal Yes/No answer is used anywhere.

## 0. Why this exists

The project has been reporting a quantity called "Nexar AP" since StudyE5 Part G, and StudyE9 / StudyE9b are built on it. **It is challenge-*style* but it is not the challenge's metric.** The difference is the negative set:

| | the project's streaming AP | the Nexar challenge (arXiv 2503.03848) |
|---|---|---|
| negatives | **every decision point of every negative clip** (20,467 of them) | **one crop per negative video** (150) |
| positives | one score per positive clip per horizon | one crop per positive video per horizon |
| chance AP | **0.00728** | **about 0.407** (classes roughly balanced) |
| horizons emphasised | h = 0 and 0.25 s | **0.5 / 1.0 / 1.5 s only** |
| headline number | AP at a chosen horizon | **mAP = unweighted mean of the three APs** |

So the two metrics differ by a factor of about 56 in chance level and look at different parts of the trajectory. This study computes **the challenge's metric exactly**, so that claims phrased as "the Nexar metric" can use Nexar's own definition. **Neither metric replaces the other**: the streaming per-decision AP remains the right instrument for a *standing query* that must decide at every tick, and it continues to be reported under its own name in `study_e9b_dense.md`. This report is the translation layer between them.

**These are not the challenge's test clips.** They are StudyD14b's 150 positive and 150 negative *validation* clips, so **no number here is leaderboard-comparable**.

## 1. The challenge's definition, and how it is instantiated here

From the paper's evaluation section: up to three crops are generated per positive video, each ending at the event time minus a horizon of 500 / 1000 / 1500 ms, and a horizon is used only when the gap between the annotated event and the alert time exceeds it; negative videos get fake event times "generated by adding Gaussian noise to half of the duration of the video"; AP is computed per horizon with scikit-learn and the three are averaged with equal weight into a single mAP.

| element | the paper | this study |
|---|---|---|
| positive crop | video ending at `time_of_event − h` | the **score at the grid point nearest `time_of_event − h`**; the horizon offset is an exact multiple of the 0.25-s step, so horizon *separation* is exact |
| horizon inclusion | only if `event − alert > h` | identical (h = 0.5 s always; h > 0.5 s only if time_of_event - time_of_alert > h (the paper's rule)) |
| negative crop | fake event = duration / 2 + Gaussian noise | identical; **the paper does not state the noise scale**, so σ = the sample standard deviation of `time_of_event − duration/2` over the 150 positive clips = **2.890 s** (population σ 2.881 s), seed 0, clipped to `[4.5 s, duration]` |
| negative alert times | assigned to match the positive distribution | **not assigned** — a stated simplification, so all 150 negatives enter every horizon |
| metric | scikit-learn AP per horizon, unweighted mean | identical (`average_precision_score`) |
| bootstrap | — | clip bootstrap 2,000, seed 0, stratified by label, shared across arms so every difference is paired |

**One difference that cannot be removed and is stated rather than hidden:** the challenge scores a ~10 s *video crop*; this study scores a *single decision* made from StudyD14b's 3-s frame window at the crop time. A model given 10 s of context could do better or worse than one given 3 s. What is reproduced exactly is the challenge's **evaluation geometry** — which moments are scored, against which negatives, with which aggregation — not its input format.

**Sensitivity:** because the negative crop times are random, every headline is also recomputed over 20 seeds (§7).

## 2. Verification recorded before any number was produced

**CLAUDE.md's mechanism verification (Steps 1–5) does not apply to this job, and that is a deliberate call, not an omission.** Steps 1–5 exist for an experiment that tests a *causal claim*: they ask which simulated quantity implements each link and whether it varies. This study makes **no causal claim**. It re-scores already-committed model outputs under a different *evaluation geometry*; there is no mechanism to be absent, zero or degenerate. StudyD14b set the precedent for exactly this case — a metric or input job records its input and control checks before computing instead, and the mechanism protocol attaches to whatever downstream study makes a claim from the numbers.

Recorded before computing: the two pre-registered controls (§9), the crop construction (§3), the σ rule, the horizon-inclusion rule, the bootstrap, and the explicit statement that the streaming metric is *not* being replaced. The CLAUDE.md 6-check consistency protocol is run in full (§10).

## 3. Crop construction

σ for the fake negative event times = **2.8905 s** (sample s.d. of `time_of_event − duration/2` over the 150 positive clips; population s.d. 2.8808 s). The paper does not state the scale, so this is pre-registered, reported, and varied over 20 seeds in §7.

| horizon | positive crops | negative crops | base rate (chance AP) | positives dropped by the alert rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.5 s | 150 | 150 | **0.5000** | 0 of 150 |
| 1 s | 106 | 150 | **0.4141** | 44 of 150 |
| 1.5 s | 66 | 150 | **0.3056** | 84 of 150 |

**The alert rule is not cosmetic.** It removes 44 of 150 positives at h = 1.0 s and 84 at h = 1.5 s, so the longer horizons are scored on a **self-selected, easier subset** — the clips whose collision was annotated as predictable that far ahead — against the full negative set. That is the challenge's own rule and is reproduced faithfully, but it means a falling AP across horizons mixes two effects: the task getting harder, and the positive population changing. Chance itself falls from 0.500 to 0.306 for the same reason.

### 8.1 What transfers between the two metrics, and what does not

A level comparison is meaningless — the chance rates differ by a factor of 56. The comparable quantity is **how far above chance an arm sits, as a multiple of how far above chance the reference arm sits**. That ratio is dimensionless and can be compared across metrics:

| contrast at h = 0.5 s | challenge: excess over chance | streaming: excess over chance | challenge ratio | streaming ratio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 32B N = 1 vs 4B N = 1, zero latency | +0.2814 vs +0.1727 | +0.0307 vs +0.0094 | **1.63x** | **3.26x** — **2x apart** |
| always-cloud 32B N = 1 vs device-only, (ii) @25 % | +0.1908 vs +0.0643 | +0.0089 vs +0.0032 | **2.97x** | **2.81x** |
| always-cloud 32B N = 3 vs 32B N = 1, (ii) @25 % | +0.1551 vs +0.1908 | +0.0055 vs +0.0089 | **0.81x** | **0.62x** |
| always-cloud 32B N = 1 vs device-only, (ii) @75 % | +0.1734 vs +0.0643 | +0.0073 vs +0.0032 | **2.70x** | **2.32x** |

**Sign and ordering transfer in every one of these contrasts. Magnitude (the excess-over-chance ratio) transfers to within 35 % in 3 of 4 and not in 1** — so the honest summary is *not* "the two metrics agree".

The 3 that agree (always-cloud 32B N = 1 vs device-only, (ii) @25 %: 2.97x vs 2.81x; always-cloud 32B N = 3 vs 32B N = 1, (ii) @25 %: 0.81x vs 0.62x; always-cloud 32B N = 1 vs device-only, (ii) @75 %: 2.70x vs 2.32x) are the contrasts where **both arms sit near chance** on both scales. The one that does not (32B N = 1 vs 4B N = 1, zero latency: 1.63x vs 3.26x) is the contrast where **the challenge metric is near its ceiling**: with 150 balanced negatives, 4B N = 1 already reaches 0.673 at h = 0.5 s, so there is little room above it and the ratio compresses toward 1. The streaming metric, with a 137:1 negative set, has no such ceiling.

**The usable rule, and the central result of the study:** *signs and orderings transfer between the two metrics unconditionally; excess-over-chance ratios transfer only while both arms are near chance; absolute AP levels never transfer.* The project's streaming AP has not been measuring something other than the challenge — it has been measuring the same orderings on a scale whose chance floor is 56x lower and whose ceiling is far away. Claims phrased as a sign or an ordering survive translation; claims phrased as a multiple need the near-chance caveat; claims phrased as an absolute AP do not transfer at all.

### 8.2 Where they agree

1. **32B beats 4B at zero latency**, with a CI excluding 0 under both metrics: challenge mAP +0.1144 [+0.0611, +0.1686]; StudyE5 Part G and StudyE9b report the same sign at these horizons.
2. **The delivered cloud beats the delivered device**: challenge mAP +0.0899 [+0.0357, +0.1497] at 25 % load, +0.0732 [+0.0179, +0.1296] at 75 %. Same sign, same load ordering, as StudyE9b.
3. **Server load costs the cloud some of its advantage**, through the hold shift alone, under both metrics.
4. **Escalation buys nothing at these horizons.** Challenge mAP against device-only: -0.0185 at trigger 10 % and +0.0079 at 20 %, **neither CI excluding 0**. StudyE9b reached the same conclusion from the other direction (its finding 7: the gate, not the resolver, is binding).

### 8.3 Where they differ — and this one matters

**StudyE9b's finding 2 — "32B N = 3 falls *below* N = 1 once delivered" — is a statement about h = 0 and h = 0.25 s, and it does not survive at the challenge's horizons.**

| metric | 32B N = 3 | 32B N = 1 | N = 3 as a share of N = 1's excess over chance |
|---|---|---|---|
| StudyE9b streaming, h = 0 | 0.0146 | 0.0384 | **23%** |
| StudyE9b streaming, h = 0.5 | 0.0128 | 0.0161 | **62%** |
| this study, challenge, h = 0.5 | 0.6551 | 0.6908 | **81%** |
| this study, challenge, mAP | 0.5504 | 0.5675 | **89%** |

At h = 0 the extra 0.5 s of hold shift costs N = 3 most of its advantage; by h = 0.5 s it has lost much less, and on the challenge's three horizons the two arms are within 0.0172 mAP of each other, with the paired CI for N = 3 against device-only (+0.0727 [+0.0035, +0.1362]) still excluding 0. **The honest statement is therefore narrower than StudyE9b's headline:** a larger payload costs most at the moment of the event and progressively less further ahead of it, so "more frames is the wrong way to spend capability" holds **for a system that must act at h ≲ 0.25 s** and is much weaker for one evaluated at 0.5–1.5 s. StudyE9b's finding is not wrong — it was computed at the horizons the streaming metric emphasises — but it should be quoted with that scope attached, and this report is the reason to do so.

A second, smaller difference: **the challenge metric compresses every gap**, because a balanced problem with 150 negatives has far less room between chance and 1.0 than a 137:1 problem. Differences that look decisive on the streaming scale (a 3x AP ratio) appear as a 1.22x ratio here. §8.1 is the correct way to compare them.

| negatives without fake alert times stand for the paper's negatives | all 150 negatives at every horizon | **a stated simplification**; it makes the negative set constant across horizons, which if anything makes the longer horizons harder, not easier |

## 11. What this shows, and what it does not

**Shows.**
1. **On Nexar's own metric, our arms land where the project said they would.** Zero-latency mAP: 4B N = 1 0.5223, 32B N = 1 0.6368, 32B N = 3 0.6493, against a chance mAP of 0.4065.
2. **Delivery costs real mAP.** 32B N = 1 falls from 0.6368 (zero latency) to 0.5675 at 25 % load and 0.5508 at 75 %; the device falls from 0.5223 to 0.4776.
3. **Signs and orderings transfer between the two metrics unconditionally; excess-over-chance ratios transfer only while both arms are near chance; absolute levels never transfer** (§8.1). The first of those three is what lets the project keep its prior conclusions.
4. **One committed finding is narrowed**: StudyE9b's "more frames is the wrong way to spend capability" is specific to h ≲ 0.25 s (§8.3).

**Does not show.**
1. **Nothing leaderboard-comparable.** These are validation clips, not the challenge's 300-clip test set, and the input is a 3-s window rather than a 10-s crop.
2. **Nothing about a model trained for this task.** Our arms are zero-shot VLM calls; the challenge's entrants train on 750 + 750 clips.
3. **No causal claim.** This is a re-scoring of committed outputs under a different evaluation geometry (§2).
4. **No horizon below 0.5 s.** The challenge does not score them, so this report cannot speak to the h = 0 and h = 0.25 s regime where several of StudyE9b's headlines live. That regime is exactly where the streaming metric remains the right instrument.
5. **The negative crop times are not the paper's.** σ is inferred, not given (§3, §7).

## 12. Verdict

**The project's "Nexar AP" was measuring the right ordering on the wrong scale, and both controls pass.** Computing the Nexar challenge's own metric exactly — one crop per clip, roughly balanced classes, horizons 0.5 / 1.0 / 1.5 s, scikit-learn AP, unweighted mAP — on our 300 validation clips reproduces **every sign and every ordering** the project previously derived from its streaming per-decision AP. **Magnitudes transfer only conditionally:** read as excess over each metric's own chance rate (0.407 here against 0.00728 there) the ratios match to within 35 % for the delivered contrasts, where both arms sit near chance, but compress by a factor of two for the zero-latency 32B-vs-4B contrast, where the balanced metric is near its ceiling (§8.1). Absolute AP levels do not transfer and never could.

**Two things change.** First, terminology: "Nexar AP" now has two precise meanings and must always be qualified as **challenge mAP** or **streaming AP**. Second, scope: StudyE9b's finding that 32B N = 3 falls below N = 1 once delivered is a statement about h ≲ 0.25 s — at the challenge's horizons N = 3 retains 89% of N = 1's excess over chance and still beats device-only with a CI excluding 0.

**No conclusion of StudyE5 Part G, StudyE9 or StudyE9b is overturned.** Controls: (a) 3,088 zero-latency crops reproduce the committed trace exactly, all within half a grid step of their target; (b) label shuffling drives mAP to 1.02–1.03x the base rate. Consistency check 6/6.
