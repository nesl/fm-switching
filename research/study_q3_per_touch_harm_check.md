**What exists today:** D162 first-token = 0.067798 + 0.00018243*uncached + 0.00015358*queued_ahead. A touch (about 5 to 7 uncached tokens) delays followers by about 0.001 s (form A, current). **Against the 2x rule:** at 1x the empirical figure is 4 to 15x above A and 4 to 13x BELOW B, so NEITHER form is within 2x (it sits near the geometric midpoint, about 0.009 s). At halved memory it is 0.5 to 0.8 of B (WITHIN 2x of B) and 30 to 70x above A. Across arms: dashcam on-time falls 0.146 per extra touch at 1x (about 1.7x B) and 0.55 at halved memory (6 to 9x B, not explained by the intercept form).
**Reading, observations only:** (1) the premise holds: the touch harm term is far too small; (2) form B is about right under memory pressure and too large at 1x, so a single per-request constant would be miscalibrated in one condition; (3) the across-arm halved-memory slope is larger than any per-request model.
**Confounding, stated plainly:** touches co-vary with load and arm (`-q2` differs from `-q` and rung 3 in more than touch count); touches in flight at a dashcam submit is endogenous (waits grow when congested, which inflates the per-request slope); the first-token interval is reconstructed from the same log that defines in-flight (self-referential); one seed, 120 windows, no independent replicate; bootstrap over windows ignores serial correlation so intervals are optimistic; within-cell slopes are diluted by window averaging.
