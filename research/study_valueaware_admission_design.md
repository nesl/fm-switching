## 0. Summary of what the data says
1. The present gate (CLOUD if est <= slack, DEFER if est <= slack + 0.5, else REJECT) is a probability-0.5 test for robots, not a certainty test: robot sends with est - slack in (-0.25, 0] were on time 12 of 25 (0.48, CI 0.30 to 0.67); in the DEFER band (0, +0.5] 9 of 23 (0.39); beyond +0.5 s 11 of 94 (0.12). Single-shot (SS) at est = slack is 0.83 (n 29); in the DEFER band 0.48 (n 25).
2. The estimate is biased low for robots (actual first token above est in 71 to 86 percent of sends, median +0.20 to +0.30 s, actual/est p50 1.25 to 1.37) and nearly unbiased for SS (actual/est p50 0.91 to 0.97), with a heavy right tail that grows with fleet load (n20 robot p90 +2.1 s on la, +5.0 s on rung 3).
3. A committed delivery-probability model already exists (v5/cf_cost.py:306 `p_on_time(residuals, predicted_s, slack_s)` = F(slack/est) on cf_service_residuals.json). On the SENT requests here it scores Brier 0.086 (robot) and 0.039 (SS) against 0.114 and 0.043 for the hard threshold and 0.087 and 0.036 for an empirical curve fitted leave-one-cell-out. So the error-distribution P needs NO new constant to be better calibrated than the step.
4. Replay at the natural break-even (P* = H / gain, H readable here = recall-only harm, hindsight): H is about 0 for almost every request, so the rule degenerates to "send unless P(on time) is about 0"; it flips 116 robot and 78 SS REJECTs to SEND across three cells (of which 29 robot and 38 SS were served on time in rung 3, 44 robot and 40 SS were late in rung 3) and 4 robot and 1 SS SENDs to REJECT. ## A. THE RULE

## C. WHAT THE RULE WOULD HAVE DONE: A REPLAY (not a result)
Script va/vad_replay.py (outputs va/vad_replay_out.txt, va/vad_replay.json; second run with P = committed F: va/vad_replay_out_committed.txt, whose header line still says LOCO), va/vad_gain_exposure.py. For each la recall opportunity the present decision is the logged one (SEND = a request row; REJECT = a `decline` event in decision.jsonl, est reconstructed at the decline time with the P replay, W from sidecar rows, n = the session's last prompt or the median earlier SS prompt, the same proxies as the B validation). New rule at P* = H / G, P_on = empirical curve from the OTHER TWO cells (both arms, per type, 10 fixed bins, shrunk then monotone), H hindsight and recall-only: the sum over other la sends submitted in the request's own estimated in-flight window of G_j x [P_j(before) - P_j(after the c x u_i shift)]; w_j = 1 (live per-device deficit weights are not in the logs). No threshold or constant was tuned; V1 (gain 1) and V2 (gain 0.3111 robot, 0.0739 SS) both reported.

Why it degenerates: H is 0 for the median request (p90 0.00 to 0.31 on-time answers) because few other recalls overlap a 1 to 3 s window and their margins are large, so P* is about 0 and the rule is "send unless P is about 0". The dashcam monitors are the other queued work (8,975, 6,882, 4,379 monitor requests per cell against about 490 to 520 recall opportunities) and are absent from H: for the V2 REJECT to SEND flips, 4.3 to 5.2 monitors were submitted inside the flipped request's in-flight window on average, and 0.7, 2.75 and 1.68 per flip had less margin (deadline minus latency) than the full queue price c x u (7, 146 and 220 monitors in total; median c x u 0.05 to 0.2 s, p90 0.2 to 1.4 s). That is exposure (an upper bound if the engine queued them behind the request), not harm.
What a replay CAN say: which logged decisions the rule's decision-time inputs would have changed (counts above); each flip's known outcome in the same opportunity under the other arm; the calibration of P_on against realised outcomes on sends. What it CANNOT say: (1) harm to others: sending changes the queue, the cache and every later decision, the logs contain only the factual branch, the engine overtakes by priority so W at submit is not the queue a later request sees (the +0.55 correlation), and monitors' on-time cannot be re-derived without a monitor P curve; (2) the outcome of a request la declined if la had sent it ("late in rung 3" is rung 3's engine state, not la's; opportunity 4451 shows the order effect: rung 3 served it only because an earlier late send had built the prefix); (3) the cache-warming value of a send (an externality on later u, opposite in sign to harm, not in the rule); (4) P_on for the declined region est > slack + 0.5, which has no la actual and a thin rung 3 sample; (5) generalisation: the fitted curve and the replay share one seed and the same 20 robot questions across the three cells (leave-one-cell-out removes the request itself, not the questions or the trace). HELD OUT before any claim: other seeds (s5 to s7) of the same cells, n24 and n32 (where B's declines were unverifiable), and the P curve fixed in advance (the committed F, no s4 fit). The replay is at the natural value only, as asked; no threshold swept.
Present-rule errors for reference (the rule must beat these, not 0): wrong rejects by cell (robot, SS): 5 and 3, 6 and 14, 20 and 22; present sends late in la: robot 5, 11, 37; SS 0, 8, 12. The n16 1x wrong rejects are 4 of 5 P errors (u under-counted), which the new rule rescues through the DEFER-band probability (P about 0.4) without correcting P.
